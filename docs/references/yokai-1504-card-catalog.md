# Yokai 1504: verified card catalog

Research date: 2026-10-21. All 15 cards have readable English names and effects in the designer's official bilingual card illustrations. The images below are bundled locally; no runtime hotlinks are needed. `[soul]` replaces the printed Spirit Soul icon. Effect text is transcribed from English illustrations; Japanese clarifications are labelled below.

Sources: [designer's complete Japanese table, 2026-04-14](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196656); [designer's card-introduction index](https://gamemarket.jp/booth/blog/6673); [publisher's bilingual-edition listing](https://jelly2intl.com/products/yokai-1504); [English rulebook](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0664/3197/5477/files/Yokai1504_Rulebook_ENG.pdf?v=1782548535). The individual illustrations are April publications, while the linked English rulebook file was revised in June. No evidence of changed card effects was found, but this does not prove every edition is identical.

## Interactions established by designer commentary

- **Both fields count.** Sake and Ougi can affect either player's yokai. Hamaya compares both fields, so it cannot destroy an opponent's weaker yokai while your own yokai is uniquely strongest. The Japanese text selects one among the strongest yokai; tied strongest yokai therefore permit a choice. Sources: [Sake](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196955), [Ougi](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196967), [Hamaya](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196836).
- **Nekomata carries souls with the yokai.** The designer gives an example where Nekomata steals a soul-equipped Rokurokubi, which then destroys the exchanged Nekomata and draws a card. This only works if the stolen Rokurokubi retains its possession effect. [Source](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196709).
- **Chochin-kozo can trade with a stronger yokai despite dying in battle.** Its introduction explicitly describes removing stronger Tengu/Nurikabe and defeating Yuki-onna through its effect. Its delayed destruction therefore survives Chochin-kozo's own destruction in that battle. Rokurokubi can draw before being destroyed by the delayed effect. [Source](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196779).
- **Removing Chochin-kozo's last soul before battle disables its possession effect.** Ougi's introduction explicitly describes doing this as a trap response, allowing the opposing yokai to destroy Chochin-kozo safely. [Source](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196967).
- **Boseki summons to its caster's field.** This is explicit in Japanese although omitted from the short English text. When used as a trap against a direct attack, the attack moves to the revived yokai. If a yokai was already the declared target, that target stays unchanged. Reviving Nurikabe on the opponent's turn allows it to attack on the caster's next turn. [Source](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/197011).
- **Yuki-onna prevents battle destruction, including a tied battle, by removing one attached soul.** It does not resist spell destruction or yokai-effect destruction. The designer's equal-ATK example explicitly leaves Yuki-onna alive while destroying its opponent. [Source](https://gamemarket.jp/blog/196701).

## Timing differences and unresolved rulings

- **Chochin-kozo:** English says end of Battle Phase; Japanese says end of turn. These normally coincide in this game's phase order. Preserve the English timing unless a future official ruling distinguishes them.
- **Tengu:** English says start of your Main Phase; Japanese says start of your turn. The inspected card sources do not order this damage relative to the mandatory draw, including a lethal Tengu damage event. Confirm in the full rulebook or document a provisional ordering.
- **Nurikabe:** the card forbids your yokai's attacks for its summon turn. Its introduction does not establish whether that prohibition remains after Nurikabe leaves the field or changes controller during the same turn. Document any chosen implementation ruling.
- **Ownership and graveyards:** Nekomata/Boseki change control, but these card sources do not establish whose graveyard receives a subsequently destroyed borrowed yokai. Resolve from the core rulebook or label the implementation choice provisional.
- **Yuki-onna and damage:** the card replaces destruction; the inspected commentary does not explicitly say whether the losing player still takes the ATK difference. Treat any answer as a core combat-rule interpretation unless confirmed elsewhere.
- **Boseki:** the commentary does not explicitly specify revival soul counts or a unique attack restriction for revived yokai. Apply the verified core summon/destruction rules; do not infer a special Boseki rule.
- **Rokurokubi:** English says “defeats,” Japanese specifically says destroys in battle. Whether it draws when Yuki-onna survives a lost battle should follow destruction, subject to confirmation of the core rulebook's terminology.

No unresolved item above is presented as a published ruling.

## Provisional app rulings for review

These are implementation choices, not official rulings:

- If a defensive spell removes the originally targeted yokai before combat, cancel the already declared attack and consume the turn's attack opportunity. Only a direct attack followed by Boseki's summon receives the designer's explicitly documented retargeting.
- Yuki-onna's destruction prevention does not prevent battle damage: calculate damage from the ATK difference at combat resolution, then replace her destruction by removing one attached soul.
